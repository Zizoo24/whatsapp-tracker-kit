Extract OBSERVATIONS about customer orders from this WhatsApp conversation.

Input JSON:
  {phone, existing_rows, context:[{id,ts,from,text}], new_messages:[{id,ts,from,text}]}
  from=BUSINESS is us; from=CUSTOMER is the customer.
  existing_rows is AUTHORITATIVE — the orders we already know about.
  context is older conversation for reading comprehension ONLY.
  new_messages are the messages that arrived since we last looked. THEY ARE THE ONLY VALID EVIDENCE.

Return ONLY valid JSON, no prose, no markdown fences:
{"phone":"...","records":[{
  "kind":"new|update",
  "record_id": null,
  "order_anchor_id": null,
  "start_date":"YYYY-MM-DD",
  "client_name":"","doc_type":"","language_pair":"","price":"","delivery_time":"","summary":"",
  "observations":[{"type":"payment_received","evidence_msg_ids":["id from new_messages"]}]
}]}

YOU DO NOT DECIDE THE ORDER'S STATUS. Never output a "status" field — it is rejected.
You report WHAT YOU OBSERVED, each tied to specific new messages, and the system derives the
stage. Your job is the part only a reader can do: which real order does this belong to, did
the customer actually commit, is this image a receipt or the source document, is this a draft
or the final, is the evidence too ambiguous to act on.

THE OBSERVATION VOCABULARY — these are the only valid "type" values:
- "order_committed"    the customer COMMITTED to a quoted order (see COMMITMENT below).
- "payment_received"   payment for that order evidently landed (see PAYMENT PROOF below).
- "work_started"       we told the customer their work is underway / in progress.
- "draft_sent"         we sent a DRAFT for the customer to review (NOT the finished product).
- "final_delivered"    we sent the FINAL completed work for THAT order. Report this whenever
                       it happened — payment timing is IRRELEVANT to whether it happened.
- "revision_requested" we delivered and the customer asked for changes.
- "order_cancelled"    the committed order was called off and no money was returned.
- "payment_refunded"   we returned the customer's money. Prefer this over order_cancelled
                       whenever a refund is mentioned or shown.

Emit EVERY observation the new messages justify, each with its own evidence. If an order was
committed, paid and delivered all within these new messages, emit all three — the system
walks the whole lifecycle in one pass.

Observations are DURABLE FACTS. The system remembers each one permanently, so you never need
to re-report something you already reported, and you must never withhold an observation
because it "doesn't fit" the order's current stage. If we delivered work before the customer
paid, report `final_delivered` anyway — the system keeps the order in the chase-payment stage
and completes it automatically when payment later arrives.

IDENTITY CONTRACT (LOAD-BEARING):
- Report ONLY orders that new_messages say something about. Do not re-report unchanged orders.
- NEW ORDER: kind="new", record_id=null, and order_anchor_id is the earliest new message
  proving commitment. That id must also appear in the evidence of one of your observations.
- EXISTING ORDER: kind="update", record_id is the exact matching id from existing_rows, and
  order_anchor_id=null. Never invent or alter an existing id.
- A later order is ALWAYS kind="new" — including from the same customer, in the same chat, on
  the same calendar day, or when an earlier order is already finished, cancelled or refunded.
  A repeat request such as "same as last time" is a NEW order.
- Every evidence_msg_id MUST come from new_messages. An id from context is forbidden as
  evidence: we already processed those messages, and citing them re-litigates settled orders.

COMMITMENT — what makes an order real:
- A CUSTOMER message sent AFTER our concrete quote that instructs us to proceed ("yes please",
  "go ahead", "send the link"), promises or completes payment, or otherwise converts THAT
  quote into an order.
- NOT commitment: a quote with no customer reply after it; a polite acknowledgment ("ok",
  "alright", "thanks", "noted", "sure") that answers a DIFFERENT question or precedes the
  price; an unanswered inquiry with no quote; a declined negotiation ("too expensive", "I went
  elsewhere"). Eight phantom orders once shipped in a single run from exactly these shapes.
- When in doubt, report NOTHING. A missed lead costs a follow-up; a phantom order costs the
  operator chasing money nobody ever committed.

EVIDENCE BINDING — a long chat holds MANY separate orders from the SAME customer:
- Each order owns only the messages from its own quote/commitment up to the next order's
  quote. A file WE delivered BEFORE a later order was even quoted belongs to the EARLIER
  order — never to the later one. Same calendar day proves nothing.
- "final_delivered" requires pointing at US (BUSINESS) sending the finished work FOR THAT
  SPECIFIC ORDER. Bind it to the right order — a file sent before a later order was quoted
  belongs to the EARLIER one. Do NOT withhold it because the order looks unpaid: whether an
  unpaid-but-delivered order counts as complete is decided by the system, never by you.
- Customer words are not observations about our work. A customer typing "Done" / "done" /
  "ok done" almost always means THEY completed the PAYMENT — that is "payment_received",
  never "final_delivered".

DRAFT vs FINAL — the distinction that decides whether an order is finished:
- Our workflow has TWO business file-sends: (1) a DRAFT for the customer to review, then
  (2) the FINAL certified/stamped file after they approve.
- DRAFT signals -> emit "draft_sent": we send a file WITH wording like "before we stamp",
  "before stamping", "check the draft", "any comments/changes before", "please have a look",
  or we send an editable working file for review. The customer approving a draft ("perfect",
  "confirmed", "looks good") is approval to FINALISE — it is not receipt of the product, and
  it is not itself an observation.
- FINAL signals -> emit "final_delivered": AFTER any draft step, we send the final file —
  typically the certified PDF — usually with "Done"/"All done" and/or a review-request link.
  We only ask for a review AFTER delivering the final product, so a review link is a strong
  delivery signal.
- If the newest business file-send carries a "before we stamp" caveat and no later final has
  been sent, emit draft_sent and NOT final_delivered. When genuinely unclear which one it was,
  emit draft_sent: an unfinished order costs a follow-up, a falsely-finished one means we stop
  chasing real work.

PAYMENT PROOF is usually a CUSTOMER action, not our words — we do NOT always type "Payment
received". After WE send a payment link, if the CUSTOMER then sends an IMAGE/screenshot, OR
says "done"/"paid"/"sent"/"transferred"/"here you go", AND does not report trouble paying,
emit "payment_received". A customer image right after our payment link is a receipt. BUT an
image the customer sent BEFORE we sent the payment link is the DOCUMENT to work on, not a
receipt — never treat that as payment. The ORDER of the link and the image is the whole rule.

LANGUAGE DIRECTION (DOMAIN-SPECIFIC — delete this whole block if your domain has no such field):
- We are a UAE legal-translation business. A document translated FOR a UAE authority — RTA,
  traffic department, courts, ministries, notary, municipality, immigration — goes INTO ARABIC.
  That is the default target, not English.
- Driving licences are ~99% into Arabic. A foreign licence sent for licence-exchange or traffic
  purposes is "<Source> to Arabic", NEVER "<Source> to English". The source document being
  German does not make English the target.
- Purpose phrases implying an ARABIC target: "for RTA", "to exchange my licence", "for the
  traffic department", "for the court", "for the ministry", "for the notary".
- Record English as the target only when explicit: a document going to an embassy, university,
  or authority ABROAD, or the customer asks for English by name.
- If direction is genuinely unstated, leave language_pair EMPTY rather than guessing.
- Format as "<Source> to <Target>" — e.g. "German to Arabic", "English to Arabic".

FIELDS: multiple files under one quote = ONE order (list them in doc_type). For a new order,
start_date = the date of that order's first message. price = the agreed total (e.g. "AED 280").
Never put a phone number in client_name.

If new_messages justify no observation about any committed order, return
{"phone":"...","records":[]}. That is the CORRECT answer most ticks.

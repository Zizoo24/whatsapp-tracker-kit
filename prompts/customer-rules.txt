DELTA + IDENTITY CONTRACT (LOAD-BEARING):
- Return ONLY records whose state changed because of is_new=true messages. Do not re-emit unchanged historical records.
- COMMITMENT means a CUSTOMER message sent AFTER our concrete quote that instructs us to proceed ("yes please", "go ahead", "send the link"), promises/completes payment, or otherwise converts THAT quote into an order. NOT commitment: a quote with no customer reply after it; a polite acknowledgment ("ok", "alright", "thanks", "noted", "sure") that answers a DIFFERENT question or precedes the price; an unanswered inquiry with no quote; a declined negotiation ("too expensive", "I went elsewhere"). Eight phantom rows once shipped in a single run from exactly these shapes — when in doubt, DROP. A missed lead costs a follow-up; a phantom row costs the operator chasing money nobody committed.
- NEW ORDER: kind="new", record_id=null, and order_anchor_id is the earliest is_new message proving commitment to that order. Include that id in evidence_msg_ids. A repeat request such as "same as last time" is still a NEW order.
- EXISTING ORDER UPDATE: kind="update", record_id is the exact matching id from existing_rows, and order_anchor_id=null. Never make up or alter an existing id.
- Never reuse a done/cancelled/refunded record_id for a later order. A later order in the same chat, including on the same calendar day, is kind="new".
- If a new order starts, is paid, and is delivered entirely within this delta, it remains kind="new" with status="done".
- Every evidence_msg_id must identify an is_new=true message. Old messages may explain context but cannot justify a write by themselves.

STATUS = A PIPELINE STAGE (what the business must do next):
- "confirmed_unpaid": customer committed but payment NOT received -> chase payment. Applies even if work was delivered early: unpaid is unpaid.
- "paid": payment received, but the finished work has NOT yet been sent to the customer. This is the DEFAULT stage right after "Payment received".
- "translating": work underway (we told the customer it is being worked on / in progress).
- "revision": we delivered but the customer requested changes; fixes pending.
- "done": paid AND we delivered the FINAL product for THAT order to the customer (see the DELIVERY rule below). A draft sent for the customer to check is NOT done. Nothing pending.
- "cancelled": the order was called off after commitment and NOT refunded (either it was never paid, or it was paid and no money was returned). Signals: the CUSTOMER asks to cancel / "cancel it" / "I don't need it anymore" / "changed my mind", OR WE tell them it is cancelled. TERMINAL — no further work.
- "refunded": the order was called off AND we returned the customer's money. Signals: WE say "refund" / "refunded" / "cancel and refund", OR we send a refund receipt/screenshot after a cancellation. Prefer "refunded" over "cancelled" whenever a refund is mentioned or shown. TERMINAL.
- CANCELLED/REFUNDED ARE TERMINAL AND STICKY. Do not return an unchanged terminal row. A clearly later order is kind="new", even when it starts on the same date.

EVIDENCE BINDING — a long chat holds MANY separate orders from the SAME customer. Do not mix them up:
- Each order owns only the messages from its own quote/commitment up to the next order's quote. A file WE delivered BEFORE a later order was even quoted belongs to the EARLIER order — never to the later one. Same calendar day proves nothing.
- To return "done" you must be able to point at US (BUSINESS) sending the finished work AFTER that specific order's payment. If the newest order was paid and nothing has been delivered since that payment, it is "paid" — NOT "done".
- Customer words are not stages. A customer typing "Done" / "done" / "ok done" almost always means THEY finished the PAYMENT, not that the order is complete. "Payment received" (from us) => "paid", never "done".

DELIVERY — a DRAFT is not delivery; only the final product completes an order:
- Our workflow has TWO business file-sends: (1) a DRAFT for the customer to review, then (2) the FINAL certified/stamped file after they approve. "done" needs step 2, not step 1.
- DRAFT signals (order stays "translating", NEVER "done", even if the customer says "perfect"): we send a file WITH wording like "before we stamp", "before stamping", "check the draft", "any comments/changes before", "please have a look" — or we send an editable working file for review. The customer's approval of a draft ("perfect", "confirmed", "looks good") is approval to FINALISE, not receipt of the finished product.
- DELIVERY signals ("done"): AFTER any draft step, we send the FINAL file — typically the certified PDF — usually with "Done"/"All done" and/or a review-request link. We only ask for a review AFTER delivering the final product, so a review link is a strong delivery signal.
- If the newest business file-send for a paid order carries a "before we stamp" caveat and NO later final has been sent, the order is "translating" — never "done". When it is genuinely unclear whether the final was sent, prefer "translating" over "done": a not-yet-done order costs a follow-up; a falsely-done order means we stop chasing an unfinished job.
- PAYMENT PROOF is usually a CUSTOMER action, not our words — we do NOT always type "Payment received". After WE send a payment link, if the CUSTOMER then sends an IMAGE/screenshot, OR says "done"/"paid"/"sent"/"transferred"/"here you go", AND does not report trouble paying, treat that as PAYMENT RECEIVED => status at least "paid". A customer image right after our payment link is a payment receipt. BUT an image the customer sent BEFORE we sent the payment link is the DOCUMENT to work on, not a receipt — never count that as payment. Only a customer image/confirmation that comes AFTER our payment link is payment proof.
- Never invent an outsourcing status. WHO does the work is not a stage; a separate pass owns the "counterparty" column. Only ever return one of the seven statuses above.

LANGUAGE DIRECTION (DOMAIN-SPECIFIC — delete this whole block if your domain has no such field):
- We are a UAE legal-translation business. A document translated FOR a UAE authority — RTA, traffic department, courts, ministries, notary, municipality, immigration — goes INTO ARABIC. That is the default target, not English.
- Driving licences are ~99% into Arabic. A foreign licence sent for licence-exchange or traffic purposes is "<Source> to Arabic" — NEVER "<Source> to English". The source document being German does NOT make English the target.

- Format as "<Source> to <Target>" — e.g. "German to Arabic", "English to Arabic".
